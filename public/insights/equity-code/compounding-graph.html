How impact is scored

Impact today is a 1–5 judgment of how much of a person's present life the harm still governs. It is interpretation, not measurement.

  • 5 — shapes where you can live, learn, borrow, or vote right now, with no effective legal remedy.
  • 4 — still operating through successor policies or unremedied losses; partial legal remedy exists.
  • 3 — formally ended or struck down, but the asset or trust it destroyed was never restored.
  • 2–1 — historical; effects are real but diffuse.

Status is separate from impact. "In force" means the rule or its direct successor still operates. "Struck, unrepaired" means a court or Congress ended it but nothing was returned. "Ended" means the mechanism stopped and some remedy followed.

How the dollars are built

Each figure carries a tag. Official is a government count or court record. Scholarly is a peer-reviewed or institutional estimate, converted to recent dollars where the source does that. Rough is an order-of-magnitude figure from contemporary reporting. No estimate means the harm is real but no one has priced it.

The running ledger sums only one-time documented extractions with a scholarly or official tag. Ongoing annual costs are totaled separately and are system-wide, not Black-community-only. Several large harms (the GI Bill, FHA underwriting, Plessy) have no defensible single number and are left out of the sum on purpose, which makes the total a floor.
