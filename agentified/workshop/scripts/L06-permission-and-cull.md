## Exercise and model Operating File entry

**Prompt:** Draft a `Permission Slip` with scope, ceiling, reversibility, escalation, expiry, authority rung, source and receipt checks, forbidden actions, retention, owner, and revocation. Then make a `Quarterly Cull` table for two invented roles: proof delivered this quarter, current owner/approver, permission validity, cost or maintenance burden, build again, and proposed disposition. All decisions remain proposals.

**Harbor example — Permission Slip:** Role: evidence-comparison preparer. Scope: read only `SYN-ROOM-01`, `SYN-ATTEND-01`, and `SYN-FAC-01` for the classroom case; produce `SYN-BRIEF-01` and trace. Ceiling/rung: prepare only; no send, booking, purchase, source edit, memory approval, or publication. Reversibility: Morgan may revise or withdraw the draft and record why. Escalation: missing tier, stale note, conflict, or staffing claim goes to Morgan as a hold. Expiry: review before the next invented planning cycle. Retention: exercise copy only until editorial review, then dispose under the course's approved process; no transfer to shared memory. Owner/approver: Morgan. Revocation: Morgan marks the proposed slip withdrawn in the paper Operating File; this does not operate a real runtime.

**Harbor example — Quarterly Cull:** Session brief role: a decision path and reviewer exist, but actual delivered value is **unproven** because the case is fictional; disposition `hold as design proposal`, no promotion. Daily weather-summary role: no reader, decision, or proof; disposition `retirement proposal for human owner`. Neither row claims a live deployment or effects a deletion.

**Claim map for source review:** The five permission fields and visible enforcement purpose derive from Chapter 9. The authority rungs and additional source/receipt/rollback prompts derive from the workbook's “Permission Slip.” The three cull tests and human-owned retirement derive from Chapter 10 and “Quarterly Cull.” Harbor permissions, retention period, and dispositions are classroom proposals, not operating policy.
