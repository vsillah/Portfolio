# Workshop photographic generation guidance

Use this checklist for AmaduTown workshop and cohort service photos. A gallery should show the named work happening, with a natural range of people and settings across the complete set.

## Before generation

- Define each service's scene role first: compact prioritization, multi-group working lab, solo recorded learning, active cohort build, or prototype test and opportunity mapping.
- Plan representation across the portfolio through age, race and ethnicity, gender presentation, body type, professional role, organization type, group size, facilitation, room, and camera angle. Do not load every identity dimension into one frame.
- Cast people as credible participants with visible work and agency. Keep their actions, tools, and decisions central. Avoid posed, interchangeable groups around laptops.
- Do not add prosthetics, robotic or augmented limbs, hearing devices, mobility equipment, or unusual physical traits merely to make a diversity prompt visible. Include disability representation only when the user explicitly requests it and the scene supports a natural, respectful, visually unambiguous depiction.
- Ask for ordinary anatomically correct hands, limbs, and devices. Keep boards, documents, and screens unlettered or indistinct; avoid generated text and logos.
- Keep AmaduTown's navy and gold mainly in the surrounding service UI, labels, and intentional small accents. Do not direct every participant's wardrobe, room, or prop toward those colors. Plan natural, varied editorial color across the full gallery and record each frame's wardrobe and environment palette.

## Image review gate

Inspect every frame at full size and in its card crop. Reject and replace frames with ambiguous or malformed anatomy, medical or assistive devices used as visual novelty, repeated faces, stock-photo posing, invented text, weak service fit, or a composition that makes a person an identity token. Compare the whole gallery before approval, including hero and secondary frames side by side. Reject a set whose people and rooms repeat the brand palette so consistently that the photos look art directed instead of observed.

Record scene, setting, camera, facilitation mode, delivery context, and casting **intent** in the source map. These fields describe the approved prompt and review goal; automated tests must not claim to determine human identity or disability from pixels.
