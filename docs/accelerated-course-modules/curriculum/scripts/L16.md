**Visible copy:** `Action · owner · evidence · date`.
```narration
Write the next action in a sentence that begins with a verb. Interview three approved participants about the first task. Review six source notes with the service owner. Test two versions of a concept with a small, eligible group. The examples are options, not prescriptions, and their numbers are exercise sizes, not customer outcomes. Choose the move that fits your actual permission and capacity. Add a person who owns it. Add a review date within the next seven days. Decide in advance what evidence you will collect and where it will be stored. Name one observation that would make you revise the idea and one condition that would stop the test. If the work touches sensitive data, pricing, access, or a promise to a customer, bring the authorized owner into the design before acting. A model can help organize the question or draft a card. It cannot grant consent, approve a change, or absorb responsibility for a result. The final chapter's call to start small becomes practical when the small step has a date and a person attached to it.
```

## Scene 4 · 03:59–05:16 · Protect the boundary
**Source:** Ch26, “What This Book Is Really About,” “The Final Question” (`c029.xhtml`). **Avatar:** off. **B-roll:** three source-status cards: observed, inferred, simulation; approved SAM art stays unaltered. **Delivery:** Speak the source category when a book case or imagined response enters.

**Visible copy:** `Observation · interpretation · simulation`.
```narration
Before you schedule the review, inspect the source labels. An observed note records what a person actually said or did under an approved process. An interpretation states what you think it means. A simulation suggests a question to ask. These three can sit in one decision file, but they cannot be merged into one claim. The same applies to the book's cases. StratQuest, VisionScope, MarketCraft, Persuasive GPT, and Sam's story belong to the book. They offered teaching frames; they do not establish that a tool is deployed here or that a result happened in your organization. If your next action depends on a result you have only imagined, move the action back to validation. If it depends on private records, keep those records in the system that is authorized to hold them. Use public, synthetic, or approved material for this workshop. Good boundaries make learning possible because the team can tell what it is allowed to use, what still needs consent, and what remains a hypothesis. That clarity makes the next decision easier to trust.
```

## Scene 5 · 05:16–06:48 · Plan the review conversation
**Source:** Ch26, “The Moment Sam Realized It Worked,” “One Year From Now,” “The Last Word” (`c029.xhtml`). **Avatar:** A03, 05:16–05:46. **B-roll:** calendar card and a decision-log entry with blank outcome field. **Delivery:** Speak the source category when a book case or imagined response enters.

**Visible copy:** `What changed your mind?`.
```narration
Put a short review on the calendar with the owner and anyone whose work or access the test affects. Send the decision question and the source links in advance. At the meeting, open with what happened, including the observations that complicate your preferred story. Compare the result with the threshold you wrote before the test. Record one of four next states: continue, revise, stop, or gather a specific missing piece of evidence. Leave the outcome field blank until the review. The book's closing image is a system that grows through repeated choices, not a final piece of software to install once. Your first loop may be a page and a calendar reminder. That is enough if the page is honest and the reminder brings people back to a real decision. If the test fails, keep the learning. If it succeeds in one context, state that context before sharing it. A result becomes reusable when its limits travel with it. Give the next owner a record they can read without guessing what you meant. The chapter's One Year From Now passage imagines a large body of feedback, experiments, and decisions. Treat those figures as a possible future in the book, never as a forecast or a result promised by this course.
```
