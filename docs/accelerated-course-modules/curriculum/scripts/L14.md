```narration
The plan is ready. The evidence ledger is organized. Yet the first person hearing the recommendation asks about delivery cost, while the second asks who benefits. Chapter Twenty-Three calls this the last-mile problem and introduces Persuasive Business Case GPT. That is a book case, not a live demonstration here. We can tailor the order in which we present them. We cannot promote an assumption into a fact because the room prefers certainty.
```

## Scene 2 · 01:22–02:44 · Hold the source steady
**Source:** Ch23, “What It Actually Does,” “Real Example: A Dashboard Optimized for Impact” (`c026.xhtml`). **Avatar:** off. **B-roll:** side-by-side pitch cards with one common evidence strip. **Delivery:** Speak the source category when a book case or imagined response enters.

**Visible copy:** `Emphasis changes; evidence stays`.
```narration
The chapter's dashboard example shows how a business case changes with the audience. It is a story in the book; we will not repeat its outcome figures as current evidence. For our fictional test, the operations pitch begins with the workload: what staff would have to record, when they would review it, and which service promise remains unchanged. The member-facing pitch begins with the experience: how a person would understand the offer, enter it, and leave without a surprise. Both versions point to the same source note and the same unknown. Neither claims that conversion will rise. Put the common evidence strip under both cards. When you rewrite a sentence, compare it line by line against that strip. If the wording becomes stronger than the record, weaken it or seek evidence. This discipline protects trust. People will disagree about which risk matters most. They should not have to wonder whether they heard two versions of the truth. The question for the pitch is simple: what must this particular person decide, and what can the source actually support?
```

## Scene 3 · 02:44–04:09 · Name the cost and rejected option
**Source:** Ch23, “A Complete Pre-Pitch Workflow,” “Common Mistakes” (`c026.xhtml`). **Avatar:** A02, 02:44–03:14. **B-roll:** synthetic choice card with current model, bounded test, and defer option. **Delivery:** Speak the source category when a book case or imagined response enters.

**Visible copy:** `Show the tradeoff before the ask`.
```narration
A persuasive pitch must make the cost visible. The shorter-commitment test could require staff training, a clearer cancellation path, and a way to handle pauses. Those are possible operating requirements in our fictional exercise, not measured costs. Put them on the card before the recommendation. Add the option to keep the current model and the option to defer until the job is verified. Why test now? Because the evidence suggests a particular mismatch worth examining. Why a bounded test? Because the economics and user experience still have open questions. The book's pre-pitch workflow asks for objections before the meeting, when they can still improve the plan. It also identifies a common mistake: asking an assistant to make a weak case sound strong. Do the reverse. Ask which sentence needs a source, which cost is missing, and which stakeholder can block the test for a good reason. Then revise the proposal. If the answer is that the test cannot be run safely or fairly, say so. A decision brief is useful when it helps the group choose, including a decision to wait.
```

## Scene 4 · 04:09–05:30 · Rehearse objections as rehearsal
**Source:** Ch23, “Prompts That Work,” “A Complete Pre-Pitch Workflow,” “What It's NOT” (`c026.xhtml`). **Avatar:** off. **B-roll:** two fictional objection cards with explicit Simulation label and a response/evidence-needed row. **Delivery:** Speak the source category when a book case or imagined response enters.

**Visible copy:** `Rehearse; then ask a person`.
```narration
A pre-pitch exercise can surface objections before the room does. Give the proposed test and its evidence to a reviewer. Ask them to challenge the weakest assumption, the delivery burden, and the risk of confusing participants. If an AI assistant produces the challenge, label it Simulation on screen. It has not spoken for operations staff or members. Turn each objection into a check with a real owner. Can the team support a pause? Ask the operator who manages enrollment. Does the offer read clearly? Test the wording with an approved participant. Would a funding partner need a different measure? Ask that partner what decision they must make. The book warns that persuasion does not replace the underlying judgment. Keep the response specific. “We have it handled” tells the room nothing. “We will review support tickets after the first cohort and stop if unresolved access questions rise” is a proposed plan that can be inspected. The precise threshold belongs in the learning plan before exposure. Rehearsal is valuable because it sharpens the next human conversation.
```

## Scene 5 · 05:30–06:46 · Write two sixty-second versions
**Source:** Ch23, “A Complete Pre-Pitch Workflow,” “The Workflow I Wish I'd Had” (`c026.xhtml`). **Avatar:** A03, 05:30–06:00. **B-roll:** two timed synthetic pitch cards; source annotations remain legible. **Delivery:** Speak the source category when a book case or imagined response enters.

**Visible copy:** `Ask clearly; cite precisely`.
```narration
Draft the first sixty-second version for the operations owner. Name the decision, the delivery change, the review date, and what would stop the test. Draft the second for the person accountable to members. Name the job, the proposed experience, the consent boundary, and what remains unverified. These are two ways into one decision. Do not write a promise that the evidence cannot carry. Annotate each claim with the source ID or mark it assumption. Where a source is absent, change the sentence to a question. Read both versions aloud. The first should sound like someone who has considered the work of implementation. The second should sound like someone who respects the burden of trying a new service. Neither should hide a condition in fine print. If the two versions lead listeners to different decisions, the pitch has drifted. Bring them back to the same bounded request. A source-linked brief lets someone disagree with your recommendation while still trusting your account of the facts.
```
