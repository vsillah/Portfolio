# Accelerated video workshop: source-faithful outline

**Audience:** product leaders, founders, and operators with one live decision or workflow to improve. **Format:** 16 self-paced videos in eight modules, in EPUB order. **Recorded runtime:** 161 minutes (2 hours 41 minutes); each estimate includes spoken teaching, a short visual example, and the exercise handoff, with about 115–145 spoken words per minute. Pause time for worksheets is additional, estimated at 60–90 minutes. A facilitator can run four 60-minute sessions by assigning each pair of modules before a 60-minute discussion and practice block; the old 60-minute/24-slide talk remains a preview, not a claim that the full book fits in an hour.

The learner carries one **decision file** through the series: a safe, synthetic or approved problem statement, evidence ledger, decision criteria, learning plan, influence map, and next-week test. Every lesson produces or revises one page of that file. Visual labels come from the existing Accelerated SAM infographic and approved Portfolio workshop plates; they explain the chapters without replacing them.

## Module 1 — Name the work (Part I: Systems)

### L01 · Build smarter, stay human — 9 min

- **Learner promise:** Recognize when faster output is outpacing the team's ability to decide and learn.
- **Book source:** Foreword; A Critical Note on Data Safety; Chapter 1, “What This Book Actually Does” and “The Quiet Part.”
- **Key concepts:** product system, human judgment, data classification, approved AI use.
- **Spoken arc:** Open on the blank “Q3 Innovation Plan” document. Name the pressure behind it. Show why one polished draft cannot settle the decision. Put the data-safety rule before the first tool example. Close with a decision the learner owns.
- **Exercise/reflection:** Identify a current decision and sort possible inputs into public, approved internal, and restricted. Use only synthetic/public/approved input in this course.
- **Required artifact:** Decision-file cover sheet plus data-use boundary.
- **Proposed visual treatment:** Book cover, then the full Accelerated SAM infographic; highlight the Signals panel while naming the three acts. No new framework graphic.
- **AmaduTown proof/demo:** Public Accelerated cover and public course/service-prototype surface only. Do not imply the prototype is the final course.

### L02 · Prioritize before generating — 10 min

- **Learner promise:** Narrow a crowded request list to one decision worth testing.
- **Book source:** Chapters 2–3; especially “The Trap That Catches Good Teams,” market attractiveness, 3C + P, strategic alternatives, and the business-model decision tree.
- **Key concepts:** priority trap, case structuring, alternatives, reversible bets.
- **Spoken arc:** Enter the meeting with fifteen “top priorities.” Ask what cannot go wrong. Apply one of the book's case frames to three options. Show the cost of an unstructured AI brainstorm. End with a choice and an explicit assumption.
- **Exercise/reflection:** Compare three synthetic options against customer pain, business value, learning value, risk, and reversibility.
- **Required artifact:** One-page priority decision with two rejected alternatives and reasons.
- **Proposed visual treatment:** Approved Signals plate as chapter opener; animate the book's 3C + P labels and the existing course-kit Priority Filter diagram as tactical overlays.
- **AmaduTown proof/demo:** A public diagnostic or workshop intake screenshot may show structured questions after a privacy-safe capture; otherwise use the synthetic option sheet.

## Module 2 — Turn questions into evidence (Part I into Part II)

### L03 · Answer-driven discovery — 9 min

- **Learner promise:** Ask for evidence that could change a named decision.
- **Book source:** Chapter 4; “The Three Questions That Change Everything,” “Make Framing Repeatable,” and the dashboard case.
- **Key concepts:** decision frame, anticipated objection, evidence gap, answer-first inquiry.
- **Spoken arc:** Start with the dashboard idea that almost died. Identify the decision owner's real question. Work backward to evidence and a test. Show how the same insight lands differently when its decision is named.
- **Exercise/reflection:** Write the decision, the answer currently believed, the strongest objection, and one missing piece of evidence.
- **Required artifact:** Decision question and evidence-gap card.
- **Proposed visual treatment:** Book dashboard case on a simple decision/evidence card; use the course-kit Discovery-to-PRD pipeline only for the transition.
- **AmaduTown proof/demo:** Public audit/diagnostic entry point can illustrate structured intake, with no claim about results or conversion.

### L04 · Build small; keep personas alive — 10 min

- **Learner promise:** Replace a static persona deck with a testable customer situation.
- **Book source:** Chapter 5, “Think Big... Build Small”; Chapter 6, “The Persona Trap” and “The Full Loop.”
- **Key concepts:** lifecycle fit, small experiment, observed behavior, dynamic persona updates.
- **Spoken arc:** Return to the empty innovation document. Choose a small test of the broad idea. Show the polished but useless persona deck, then revise a persona from fresh evidence and mark what remains unknown.
- **Exercise/reflection:** Rewrite one persona as job, context, observed signal, uncertainty, and next validation question.
- **Required artifact:** Living persona card with source and review date.
- **Proposed visual treatment:** Approved Signals plate yields to the book's persona before/after; use readable text cards rather than a new decorative avatar.
- **AmaduTown proof/demo:** Use a synthetic persona card. Any diagnostic response or client persona requires separate permission and redaction.

## Module 3 — Make signal travel (Part II: Align)

### L05 · From feedback to signal — 10 min

- **Learner promise:** Separate repeated noise from a pattern strong enough to affect a decision.
- **Book source:** Chapter 7; “Structure Your Raw Feedback,” the “Just Make It Faster” case, and “The Full Discovery Loop.”
- **Key concepts:** traceable input, clustering, jobs, segment, evidence strength.
- **Spoken arc:** Start with 300 comments and no decision. Cluster a small synthetic sample, preserve contrary evidence, then translate one theme into a testable opportunity. Show where human review corrects a confident summary.
- **Exercise/reflection:** Code six synthetic feedback snippets, name one pattern, and flag one counterexample.
- **Required artifact:** Feedback-to-signal ledger with input IDs, pattern, counterexample, and proposed decision.
- **Proposed visual treatment:** Approved Signals plate and existing Feedback-to-Signal Funnel brief; preserve source IDs on screen.
- **AmaduTown proof/demo:** Value Evidence Pipeline documentation is implementation evidence, not public outcome proof. Show only a locally built synthetic fixture or approved/redacted capture.

### L06 · Roadmap to PRD, without drift — 11 min

- **Learner promise:** Keep a roadmap bet connected to its evidence through the development handoff.
- **Book source:** Chapter 8, “Why Most Roadmaps Fail” and institutional memory; Chapter 9, “Build Comprehension Over Completion,” “Pre-Test Your Handoff,” and “Redefine Done.”
- **Key concepts:** tradeoffs, outcome commitment, PRD comprehension, scope and metric continuity.
- **Spoken arc:** Put three roadmap options on a wall. Choose one with criteria rather than rank theater. Follow it into a PRD, then ask a developer to explain the expected behavior back. Record the change before building.
- **Exercise/reflection:** Trace one bet from evidence to PRD acceptance criteria and a measure of success.
- **Required artifact:** Roadmap/PRD trace card with source, decision, owner, scope, exclusions, and measure.
- **Proposed visual treatment:** Approved Alignment plate; tactical roadmap-to-PRD line from the existing slide and diagram briefs.
- **AmaduTown proof/demo:** Public product/feature description may show the intended outcome; use a synthetic PRD for the handoff sequence.

## Module 4 — Make the roadmap learn (Part II: Align)

### L07 · Commit to learning — 10 min

- **Learner promise:** Put learning checkpoints beside delivery dates.
- **Book source:** Chapter 10, “Ship Learning, Not just Products,” and Chapter 11, Sam's “Push to Pull” story.
- **Key concepts:** learning track, review rhythm, shared outcome language, pull from evidence.
- **Spoken arc:** Contrast a quarter of promised features with a quarter of answerable questions. Build two checkpoints. Use Sam's changed team language as a narrative illustration, clearly identified as the book's story.
- **Exercise/reflection:** Convert a delivery milestone into a learning question with a review date and possible adjustment.
- **Required artifact:** Two-checkpoint learning roadmap.
- **Proposed visual treatment:** Approved Alignment plate plus the book's learning track; no invented maturity staircase.
- **AmaduTown proof/demo:** A public roadmap claim is unnecessary. Use the learner's sanitized example or a synthetic roadmap.

### L08 · Metrics and the ten-part learning plan — 12 min

- **Learner promise:** Write a test that can change course when results arrive.
- **Book source:** Chapter 12, “The Metrics That Matter”; Chapter 13, “The Ten Components.”
- **Key concepts:** outcome measures, leading signals, success criteria, timing, tripwires, pivots, hypothesis backlog.
- **Spoken arc:** Start with the dashboard that looked healthy while the product failed. Pick one measure tied to the decision. Walk through the book's ten components in two passes: the decision and test first, then review, tripwires, and next move.
- **Exercise/reflection:** Complete a small learning plan using all ten book fields; record a stop or pivot threshold before seeing results.
- **Required artifact:** Ten-component learning plan and metric definition.
- **Proposed visual treatment:** Approved Alignment plate; show the ten book fields in a readable worksheet and the existing Learning System Loop only after the fields are complete.
- **AmaduTown proof/demo:** Public/synthetic analytics screen can illustrate metric definitions. Internal dashboards require a reviewed capture and must not imply verified customer results.

## Module 5 — Read the decision room (Part II: Align)

### L09 · Map the decision theater — 11 min

- **Learner promise:** See who can move, slow, or stop a product decision and why.
- **Book source:** Chapters 14–15; “The Strategic Argument Blueprint,” power/interest mapping, and “The Shuttle Diplomacy Playbook.”
- **Key concepts:** authority, incentives, risk, stakeholder interests, informal decision path.
- **Spoken arc:** Begin with a sound recommendation that receives a slow no. Map the visible meeting and the decisions happening outside it. Rebuild the argument around the actual risks and sequence of conversations.
- **Exercise/reflection:** Map four role types using fictional names, their interests, objections, and the evidence each needs.
- **Required artifact:** Decision theater map and pre-meeting sequence.
- **Proposed visual treatment:** Book's power/interest matrix with the existing Decision Theater Map brief; approved Alignment plate opens the chapter.
- **AmaduTown proof/demo:** Use a synthetic service proposal. No internal contact names or sales conversations.

### L10 · Influence before impact — 9 min

- **Learner promise:** Make a responsible case for a choice without hiding its tradeoffs.
- **Book source:** Chapter 16; notification-settings example, shuttle diplomacy, and the pitch checklist.
- **Key concepts:** stakeholder-specific proof, emotional motivators, tradeoffs, objections, trust.
- **Spoken arc:** Retell the proposal that did not land. Reframe it for the customer, engineering, and executive concerns. Show the revised pitch and its explicit cost/risk. End at the decision, not at applause.
- **Exercise/reflection:** Draft a 90-second recommendation for one decision owner and state what would change your mind.
- **Required artifact:** Stakeholder-specific decision brief.
- **Proposed visual treatment:** Approved Alignment plate with annotated pitch cards; avoid invented persuasion statistics.
- **AmaduTown proof/demo:** A public service page can show positioning, while the actual decision brief remains synthetic.

## Module 6 — Turn habits into a system (Part III: Momentum)

### L11 · From the shift to a repeatable loop — 10 min

- **Learner promise:** Connect a good decision to the next one so the team does not relearn the same lesson.
- **Book source:** Chapter 17, Sam's changed meetings; Chapter 18, “Signal → Insight,” “Insight → Test,” “Test → Decision,” and “Decision → Repeatable Momentum.”
- **Key concepts:** operating rhythm, experiment record, memory, automation boundary, tool sprawl.
- **Spoken arc:** Open with Sam's shorter roadmap meeting. Trace the unseen work that made it possible. Build one signal-to-decision path and choose where automation helps and where a person must review.
- **Exercise/reflection:** Draw a current workflow and mark its missing review or memory handoff.
- **Required artifact:** Signal-to-decision loop map with owner and review gate.
- **Proposed visual treatment:** Approved Momentum plate and the existing Learning System Loop; use the book's arrow sequence as labels.
- **AmaduTown proof/demo:** Public service prototype can illustrate a flow. Internal n8n or agent screens require a sanitized, approved capture.

### L12 · Jobs and synthetic signals — 11 min

- **Learner promise:** Use AI-assisted synthesis and simulation as hypotheses that still need real-world checking.
- **Book source:** Chapter 19, ClientQ; Chapter 20, SudoPoll, including statistical validity, disclosure, and validation checkpoints.
- **Key concepts:** jobs from feedback, simulation limits, counterexamples, validation with people.
- **Spoken arc:** Convert a synthetic feedback bundle into jobs. Then simulate objections to a proposed change. Separate what the simulation suggests from what actual customers have said. End with a validation checkpoint.
- **Exercise/reflection:** Write one job statement and two simulation questions; list the real evidence required before a launch choice.
- **Required artifact:** Jobs card and synthetic-signal caveat/validation card.
- **Proposed visual treatment:** Approved Momentum plate; book's ClientQ/SudoPoll labels appear as attributed case titles, with a persistent “simulation” label for model output.
- **AmaduTown proof/demo:** No verified live ClientQ or SudoPoll demo is in this source set. Use a synthetic workbook; do not present book cases as shipped tools.

## Module 7 — Test the strategic story (Part III: Momentum)

### L13 · Strategic shifts and future concepts — 11 min

- **Learner promise:** Explore a business-model change and a product story before committing resources.
- **Book source:** Chapter 21, StratQuest; Chapter 22, VisionScope and MarketCraft.
- **Key concepts:** model alternatives, concept narrative, segment objection, test design.
- **Spoken arc:** Start with flat metrics and a call to pivot. Compare three options with operational costs. Draft a future-state story, then challenge it from different customer contexts. Mark the next real test.
- **Exercise/reflection:** Choose one business-model alternative and write its smallest falsifiable test; draft a 100-word concept explanation.
- **Required artifact:** Strategic alternatives sheet and concept-test card.
- **Proposed visual treatment:** Approved Momentum plate; use the book's named-tool case cards and the existing business-model decision tree.
- **AmaduTown proof/demo:** Use a synthetic concept. No live StratQuest, VisionScope, or MarketCraft product claim without a verified asset.

### L14 · The last mile of influence — 8 min

- **Learner promise:** Adapt one evidence-backed proposal to the audience without changing the evidence.
- **Book source:** Chapter 23, Persuasive GPT, including stakeholder versions and pre-pitch workflow.
- **Key concepts:** audience, objection handling, pre-mortem pitch, evidence integrity.
- **Spoken arc:** The plan is sound, yet the room is unconvinced. Rewrite the same recommendation for two stakeholders. Compare what changes in emphasis and what stays anchored to the source. Invite a pre-pitch challenge.
- **Exercise/reflection:** Draft two 60-second versions and flag any sentence that outruns the evidence.
- **Required artifact:** Two-audience pitch card with source annotations.
- **Proposed visual treatment:** Approved Momentum plate; book's Persuasive GPT case as labeled example and side-by-side pitch cards.
- **AmaduTown proof/demo:** A public proposal template can illustrate formatting only; do not show private deals or assert the named tool is live.

## Module 8 — Scale only what learns (Part III: Momentum)

### L15 · Governance and the system that learns — 12 min

- **Learner promise:** Expand a useful loop across teams without losing its evidence, owner, or stop condition.
- **Book source:** Chapter 24, fragmented experiments, metric collisions, governance, and scaling mistakes; Chapter 25, before/after operating rhythm.
- **Key concepts:** shared experiment template, decision log, review cadence, institutional memory, scale risk.
- **Spoken arc:** A successful local test attracts requests to “do this everywhere.” Show what breaks at that point. Add common records, ownership, and review. Return to Sam's end-of-day scene as a book illustration, not a verified AmaduTown outcome.
- **Exercise/reflection:** Choose one rule that a second team would need before reusing the test.
- **Required artifact:** Scale-readiness checklist and decision-memory record.
- **Proposed visual treatment:** Approved Momentum plate and the existing Learning System Loop; the book's five scaling problems become labels on the checklist.
- **AmaduTown proof/demo:** Portfolio repository docs can demonstrate a documented review gate. Live operating outcomes need separate measurement evidence.

### L16 · What comes next — 8 min

- **Learner promise:** Leave with one small, accountable learning move for the next week.
- **Book source:** Chapter 26, “What This Book Is Really About,” “The Final Question,” and “One Year From Now.”
- **Key concepts:** pain-led next step, review date, human ownership, learning that compounds.
- **Spoken arc:** Revisit the decision from L01. Show how its evidence, test, and influence map changed. Choose one action for the next seven days, a person who reviews it, and a condition that stops or changes it. End with the book's unfinished-system premise.
- **Exercise/reflection:** Complete the one-week plan and schedule a review with an actual owner.
- **Required artifact:** One-week learning commitment and assembled decision file.
- **Proposed visual treatment:** Full Accelerated SAM infographic; highlight the learner's path through Signals, Alignment, and Momentum, then return to the book cover.
- **AmaduTown proof/demo:** Course artifact itself is the proof of learning. No performance claim or sales call is required.

## Assembly for a facilitated cohort

| Session | Assigned videos | Live practice (60 min) | Leave with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Modules 1–2 | Compare a decision frame and safe input boundary in pairs; facilitator challenges the evidence gap. | Decision question and living persona. |
| 2 | Modules 3–4 | Code shared synthetic feedback, trace it to a roadmap/PRD, and critique metric definitions. | Feedback ledger and learning plan. |
| 3 | Modules 5–6 | Role-play the decision theater and inspect a signal-to-decision loop. | Influence map and review gate. |
| 4 | Modules 7–8 | Pressure-test a concept and business model, then commit to a one-week test. | Assembled decision file. |

The cohort uses the same lesson videos and worksheets. Live time adds discussion and feedback; it does not replace omitted chapters with a compressed lecture.
